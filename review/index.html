Who Benefits From You Being Ill In Britain?

2026-05-21 health, AI, policy

Britain talks about healthcare as if illness simply arrives at the NHS door. Some of it does: ageing, genetics, infection, injury, grief and chance are real. But a large amount of poor health is commercially shaped long before it reaches a GP, pharmacy, ambulance or hospital ward.

Food environments are designed. Alcohol is promoted. Gambling products are built for repeat engagement. Nicotine markets keep evolving. Research is funded. Charities are sponsored. Political access is cultivated. Regulatory loopholes are defended.

This is the sickness economy: the network of companies, funders, charities, researchers, lobbyists, professional bodies and policy channels that benefit when preventable illness is normalised, treated, insured, medicated, outsourced or managed rather than avoided.

The question: who profits when Britain is sick, and how close are they to the evidence, institutions and policies meant to keep us well?

The NHS is left holding much of the bill. The 2025 NHS 10 Year Health Plan for England says the NHS must shift from sickness to prevention. That is right. But prevention will remain weak unless Britain also confronts the commercial systems that profit from preventable illness. GOV.UK

Industry-funded education and charity partnerships are not automatically dishonest. But they create a structural conflict when the funder has a financial interest in the behaviour being discussed. This is not an allegation of dishonesty; it is a disclosure and governance problem.

Drinkaware says it is funded by voluntary donations from alcohol producers, pub operators, restaurants, supermarkets and retailers. Who Paid For The Evidence?

Research has a supply chain. A study can be biased without being fabricated. Bias can enter through the question selected, the comparator chosen, the follow-up period, the outcome measured, access to the data, whether negative findings are published, who writes the paper, and how the abstract frames the results.

Cochrane

Drug and device studies sponsored by manufacturing companies had more favourable efficacy results and conclusions than studies with other sponsorship sources. Source

PLOS Medicine

Nutrition reviews with food or drink industry conflicts were five times more likely to find no positive association between sugary drinks and weight gain. Source

Health Research Authority

In April 2026, HRA reported that two thirds of trials completed in 2023 had not yet published, or told HRA they had published, results on a public registry. Source

Policy implication

Every important health claim used in policy should carry a provenance label: funder, sponsor role, registry ID, protocol, comparator, data owner, analysis, conflicts and publication status.

The Fragmented Register Problem

flowchart LR A["Health claim"] --> B["Study"] B --> C["Funder"] C --> D["Company / trade body"] D --> E["Charity or patient group"] D --> F["APPG or ministerial meeting"] D --> G["Consultation submission"] G --> H["Policy or guideline"] E --> H B --> H

Relevant records are scattered across NHS conflict registers, ABPI Disclosure UK, Charity Commission filings, Companies House, APPG registers, MPs' interests, ministerial meetings, lobbying records, NICE declarations, trial registries and journal disclosures.

The UK consulted on mandatory disclosure of medicines and medical-device payments to the healthcare sector, but the December 2025 government response chose a guidance-based approach rather than immediate statutory reporting. GOV.UK

Even when information exists, it is often declared but obscure.

What AI Should Do

AI should not only help hospitals process illness faster. It should help Britain see why preventable illness keeps being produced. The public-interest AI tool would not make accusations. It would produce evidence cards.

A Better UK For Healthcare

This is not an anti-business agenda. It is an anti-obscurity agenda.

  1. industry-free public health education in schools
  2. clear funding labels on health charities and campaigns
  3. provenance labels for research used in policy
  4. full trial registration and results reporting
  5. joined-up conflict registers for health policy
  6. stronger rules on brand advertising, outdoor advertising and sports sponsorship
  7. transparent governance for the statutory gambling levy
  8. AI tools for public-interest transparency
Health policy should be shaped by public interest, not obscured influence.

The First Investigation

Start narrow. Pick one NICE guideline or technology appraisal.

flowchart LR A["Guideline claim"] --> B["Cited studies"] B --> C["Study funders"] C --> D["Author conflicts"] D --> E["Patient group submissions"] E --> F["Manufacturer payments"] F --> G["Evidence card"]

If that works, expand to food, alcohol, gambling and nicotine policy claims.

The public should be able to ask one question: Who paid for that claim?
